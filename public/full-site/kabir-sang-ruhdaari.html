We have reached so far, enjoying the cacophony on the outside and ignoring the symphony inside. — Kabir Sang Ruhdaari

The gathering

Once a year, on the full moon of Jyestha, the laboratory gathers around the voices of Kabir, Meera, Bulleh Shah, Raidaas and Lalon Fakir — original compositions and folk songs, sung in a room that sings back.

It is not a cultural presentation. It is a shared space of reflection. Come as you are. Sing along. Paint. Breathe.

While the musicians play, painters work at the edge of the stage — the evening leaves behind canvases, and the artworks stay with the collective, a growing archive of what each Ruhdaari looked like from the inside.

The gathering is contribution-based and open through registration alone — no tickets, no rows of passive seats.

The evening holds

Song
The repertoire

Fifteen and more compositions — dohas, folk tunes and originals — carried from edition to edition and sung with the room.

Paint
Live painting

Painters respond to the songs as they happen. The canvases remain with PTL after the night ends.

Breath
Reflection

Between songs, silence is allowed to do its work. Nobody is only an audience here either.
